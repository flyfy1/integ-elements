Listen and repeat

One element at a time: hear it in a US or UK voice, see the stressed syllable and IPA, record yourself and compare. Starts with the hardest names.

  • Beryllium: The stress is on the second syllable: buh-RIL-ee-um.
  • Nitrogen: The first i sounds like 'eye' (NY-truh-jun), and the last syllable is a weak 'jun', not a full 'gen'.
  • Fluorine: The 'uo' is one sound, so the word has two syllables with stress first: FLOOR-een.
  • Magnesium: Magnesium looks like manganese, so learners often mix up the two names.
  • Aluminium / Aluminum: Learners mix the two versions: US is a-LOO-mi-num (4 syllables), UK is al-yoo-MIN-ee-um (5 syllables).
  • Phosphorus: The stress is on the first syllable, FOS-fuh-rus, and the second 'o' is weak, not a full 'or' sound.
  • Potassium: The stress falls on the second syllable: puh-TAS-ee-um.
  • Manganese: Manganese and magnesium look alike, so learners often say one when they mean the other.
  • Iron: Iron is said EYE-ern (UK EYE-uhn); the o is not pronounced as a full vowel.
  • Yttrium: The first Y is a vowel, so the name starts with the sound /ɪ/ (IT-ree-um), with no 'y' sound in front.
  • Molybdenum: The stress falls on the second syllable, mo-LIB-duh-num, and the 'e' is reduced to a weak 'uh'.
  • Technetium: The 'ch' is a hard /k/ sound, the stress is on 'NEE', and 'ti' sounds like 'sh': tek-NEE-shee-um.
  • Ruthenium: The th is a soft /θ/ sound and the stress falls on the second syllable: roo-THEE-nee-um.
  • Palladium: The stress falls on the second syllable LAY: puh-LAY-dee-um.
  • Antimony: The stress falls on the first syllable, AN-ti-moh-nee, not the second.
  • Iodine: It starts with the sound 'eye' and the stress is on it: EYE-uh-dine (US) or EYE-uh-deen (UK).
  • Xenon: An x at the start of an English word is said /z/, so xenon begins like 'zee'.
  • Caesium / Cesium: The 'ae' is said like 'ee', the c is /s/ and the s is /z/: SEE-zee-um.
  • Lanthanum: The stress falls on the first syllable (LAN-thuh-num), and 'th' is the soft /θ/ sound, not a 't'.
  • Praseodymium: The y is a short 'i' as in 'dim', the s sounds like z, and the main stress falls on DIM: pray-zee-oh-DIM-ee-um.
  • Promethium: The th is the tongue-between-teeth /θ/ sound, not s: pro-MEE-thee-um.
  • Europium: Unlike 'Europe', the stress falls on the second syllable: yoo-ROH-pee-um.
  • Dysprosium: The y is a short 'i' as in 'dis', and the s in -sium sounds like /z/: dis-PROH-zee-um.
  • Ytterbium: The first 'y' is a vowel /ɪ/, not a 'y' consonant, and stress falls on the second syllable: ih-TUR-bee-um.
  • Lead: The metal is said /led/ like 'red'; 'leed' is the verb meaning 'to guide'.
  • Thorium: The 'th' is /θ/, made with the tongue between the teeth, not an 's' or 't' sound.
  • Protactinium: The stress falls on the third syllable: pro-tak-TIN-ee-um.
  • Americium: The stress falls on 'ri' and 'ci' sounds like 'sh': am-uh-RISH-ee-um.
  • Mendelevium: The stress falls on the third syllable, men-de-LEE-vee-um, and the 'e' there is a long 'ee'.
  • Rutherfordium: The th is a voiced /ð/ (tongue between teeth), not s or z; say ruh-ther-FOR-dee-um.
